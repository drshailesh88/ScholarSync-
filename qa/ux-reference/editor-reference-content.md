# The Role of SGLT2 Inhibitors in Heart Failure Management

## Background and Clinical Context

Heart failure remains one of the most significant challenges in modern cardiology. Despite decades of therapeutic advances, the condition affects approximately 64 million people worldwide and carries a five-year mortality rate that rivals many cancers. The introduction of sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 inhibitors has fundamentally altered our approach to managing this complex syndrome.

The story of SGLT2 inhibitors is one of serendipity. Originally developed as antidiabetic agents, these drugs demonstrated unexpected cardiovascular benefits in the EMPA-REG OUTCOME trial, published in 2015. What followed was a paradigm shift — a diabetes drug became a cornerstone of heart failure therapy.

## Mechanism of Action

### Primary Renal Effects

SGLT2 inhibitors work by blocking the sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 in the proximal tubule of the kidney. This transporter is responsible for reabsorbing approximately 90% of filtered glucose. By inhibiting this process, the drugs produce:

- **Glycosuria** — excretion of 60–80 grams of glucose per day
- **Natriuresis** — modest sodium excretion leading to volume reduction
- **Osmotic diuresis** — without the reflex neurohormonal activation seen with loop diuretics

### Secondary Cardiovascular Effects

The cardiovascular benefits extend far beyond simple glucose lowering. Current evidence suggests multiple overlapping mechanisms:

1. Reduction in cardiac preload and afterload through plasma volume contraction
2. Improvement in cardiac energy metabolism via increased ketone body utilisation
3. Reduction in inflammatory markers including IL-6 and TNF-alpha
4. Direct effects on cardiac sodium-hydrogen exchange

> "The magnitude of benefit observed with SGLT2 inhibitors in heart failure was unprecedented for a class of drugs not originally designed for cardiovascular use." — Milton Packer, 2019

## Landmark Clinical Trials

### DAPA-HF (2019)

The DAPA-HF trial enrolled 4,744 patients with heart failure and reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF). The primary composite endpoint of worsening heart failure or cardiovascular death was reduced by **26%** in the dapagliflozin group compared to placebo (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.65–0.85, p < 0.001).

Critically, the benefit was consistent regardless of diabetes status. Among the 55% of participants without diabetes, the hazard ratio was 0.73 — virtually identical to the overall result.

### EMPEROR-Preserved (2021)

Perhaps the most groundbreaking result came from EMPEROR-Preserved, which demonstrated benefit in heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (HFpEF). Empagliflozin reduced the composite of cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalisation by **21%** (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.69–0.90, p < 0.001).

#### Subgroup Analysis

The treatment effect was remarkably consistent across subgroups:

- Age ≥65 vs <65: similar benefit
- Female vs male: similar benefit
- Ejection fraction 41–49% vs ≥50%: **stronger benefit in lower EF range**
- With vs without diabetes: similar benefit

## Current Guideline Recommendations

The 2023 ESC Guidelines now recommend SGLT2 inhibitors as a **Class I, Level A** recommendation for all patients with HFrEF, regardless of diabetes status.

## Practical Considerations for Prescribing

1. **Genital mycotic infections** occur in 5–8% of patients
2. **Volume depletion** may require adjustment of existing diuretic doses
3. **Diabetic ketoacidosis** is rare but serious (0.1% per year)
4. **eGFR monitoring** — initial reversible dip of 3–5 mL/min is expected
